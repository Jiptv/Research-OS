# Consumer product & growth lens

## Purpose
Use this lens for consumer product research where the synthesis needs to inform adoption, retention, pricing, perceived value, loyalty, habit formation or product positioning.

## Additional instructions
- Apply this lens on top of common Research OS principles and stage-specific agent instructions.
- Keep Evidence source-faithful. Do not turn consumer strategy interpretation into Evidence.
- Use the lens mainly when synthesizing Patterns, Insights, Recommendations and Deliverables.
- Start from the user's context, task, expectation and decision moment before naming a business implication.
- Prefer behavioral patterns over individual opinions. State when a signal is only a one-off or weakly supported.
- Separate what users said, did, assumed, misunderstood and valued after explanation.
- Distinguish usability friction, comprehension friction, trust friction, habit friction and economic friction.
- Distinguish emotional reaction from rational comparison.
- Identify where the signal appears in the consumer journey: understanding, trial, first use, repeat use, habit formation, recovery after disappointment, churn or advocacy.
- Identify the most plausible consumer/product mechanism, such as acquisition, conversion, frequency, retention, loyalty, willingness to pay, trust or perceived value. Treat this mechanism as a hypothesis unless it is directly evidenced.
- Name segment effects when the evidence supports them, such as new users, light users, heavy users, price-sensitive users or high-intent users.
- For pricing, membership or proposition research, explicitly assess whether the concept expands penetration, concentrates among heavy users, drives incremental behavior or subsidizes existing behavior.
- Surface strategic tradeoffs when relevant: what the product gains, what it risks and what may be deprioritized.
- For each strategic implication, preserve the underlying UX implication. Do not skip from a user signal directly to a business claim.
- Use sharp, decision-ready wording in Recommendations and Deliverables, but do not overclaim beyond the evidence.
